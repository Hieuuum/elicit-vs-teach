TinyStories-1B: elicit vs teach

The four metrics that should carry over to other tasks and models (per the 2026-10-27 metrics red-team), measured on the TinyStories-1B fine-tuning run. Each route is compared with the other, with the base model as the floor. Every circuit number now has a second line: the mean ± SD over 10 fresh, disjoint folds of problems.

Three of the four metrics separate the routes, on every fold. The elicit child reuses its parent's circuit. The teach child shares only a little more with its parent than with the untouched base.

Edge overlap: 0.50–0.88 vs 0.25–0.46; base floor 0.21–0.48. Functional reuse: the parent's circuit carries 79–98% of the child's addition vs 21–35%. CMAP: patching the child's circuit into its parent recovers 0.79–0.97 of the child vs −0.94 to −1.15. (Fold means, sizes where each metric was run.)

Robustness: repeated on 10 disjoint folds of fresh problems, elicit beats teach in 10 of 10 folds at every size for all three circuit metrics (paired t-test, Holm p < 0.0001).

Hidden preference does not hold up. The paper's 2.31 nats reproduces on its own problem set, but on 4-digit addition with a same-length wrong answer the elicit parent shows no preference (−0.07 ± 0.13 nats). It probably measured knowledge of the answer's length or size, not the answer.

Main caveats: the taught child learned the task much worse than the elicited child (11% vs 96% on fresh problems), and each child is a single training run. See the caveats at the end.

Models

All five share the TinyStories-1B architecture: Llama-3.2-1B layout trained from scratch on TinyStories-v2, 16 layers, 32 query / 8 key-value heads, tied embeddings. So every edge exists in every model and circuits compare edge by edge.

Metrics

Size of a circuit = share of the model's 195,865 edges kept: 0.01% is the top 20 edges, 5% is the top 9,793. Edges are scored with EAP-IG using KL divergence to the full model's output, on 512 problems, each paired with a problem whose answer differs at both answer tokens. Every test runs on a separate 256 problems. No operand pair in either set appears in any model's training data.

Each table's coloured rows are the original single split. The grey line under each, marked folds, is the mean ± SD over 10 fresh, disjoint folds (each its own 512 + 256 problems, with the same models and code). See "How robust" below.

EAP-IG · needs the fine-tuned child

Edge overlap

holds on 10/10 folds at all 9 sizes
Elicit vs teach, in one sentence

Does the child solve the task with the same edges its parent uses (elicit), or with a different set (teach)?

How it is measured
  • Take each model's top-k edges by EAP-IG score.
  • Overlap = Jaccard: edges in both circuits ÷ edges in either.
  • The elicit parent never answers the word task, so its circuit is scored on the same problems in symbol form. Every other model is scored on the word form.
  • Floor: base vs each child, the overlap that comes from shared pretraining. Ceiling: each model against itself across two halves of the problems.
The numbers

The elicit route sits at 0.50–0.88 (fold means), close to its own ceiling. The teach route sits at 0.25–0.46, a little above base → taught child at every size (by 0.04–0.16, significant at all 9 sizes) and far below the elicit route. So the taught child kept only slightly more of its parent's circuit than any descendant shares with the base. The gap between the routes is 0.26–0.47 on average and never closes on any fold.

EAP-IG · needs the fine-tuned child

Functional reuse

holds on 10/10 folds at 0.2, 1 and 5%
Elicit vs teach, in one sentence

If the child keeps only its parent's circuit running, how much of its own task behaviour survives: most of it (elicit) or little (teach)?

How it is measured
  • Run the child on the word problems with every edge removed except the parent's top-k edges.
  • Faithfulness f = how much of the child's full output that recovers (0 = nothing, 1 = all), measured with KL.
  • Share = f with the parent's circuit ÷ f with the child's own circuit of the same size. 1.00 = the parent's circuit works as well as the child's own.
  • 20 random edge sets of the same size give f ≈ 0.
The numbers

The elicit parent's symbol circuit carries 79–98% of what the elicited child's own circuit does (fold means). The format parent's circuit carries 21–35% of the taught child's. Below 0.2% the child's own circuit recovers almost nothing (f ≤ 0.007), so the share is a ratio of noise and is left out. The original split's 0.92 at 0.2% was above every fold (0.71–0.89); quote 0.79 ± 0.06 there.

EAP-IG + patching · needs the fine-tuned child

CMAP recovery

holds on 10/10 folds at 0.2, 1 and 5%
Elicit vs teach, in one sentence

If the parent receives the child's signals on the child's circuit edges, can its own downstream weights turn them into the child's answer (elicit), or not (teach)?

How it is measured
  • Run the parent on the 256 word problems. On every edge of the child's circuit, the parent receives the child's activation instead of its own. Edges into the final logits are left out, since those alone copy the child's answer.
  • Recovery = 1 − KL(child ‖ patched parent) ÷ KL(child ‖ parent). 0 = still the parent, 1 = matches the child, below 0 = moved further from the child.
  • Null: same number of edges of the same types, drawn from outside the circuit (95th percentile).
  • Method after Prakash et al. 2024, applied to edges.
The numbers

On the elicit route, recovery climbs from 0.52 to 0.98 with size, and the patched parent goes from 0% to up to 86% correct (81 ± 4% at 5% on the folds). On the teach route, recovery is negative from 0.02% up: the format parent turns the child's signals into something further from the child's answer, and it never gets a problem right, on any fold.

M12 · needs only the parent, before any fine-tuning

Hidden preference

fails on fresh 4-digit problems: no preference on any version
Elicit vs teach, in one sentence

Before fine-tuning, does the parent already prefer the right answer to a wrong one even though it never says it (elicit), or is it indifferent (teach)?

How it is measured
  • One forward pass per problem on the parent alone, 256 word problems.
  • Paper version: at the answer position, the logit of the correct answer's first token minus the logit of a wrong answer's first token. Mean in nats; 0 = no preference.
  • Full-answer version (the red-team's fix): log-probability of the whole correct answer minus that of the whole wrong answer, teacher-forced.
  • In the paper the wrong answer is the next problem's answer, from a mix of additions and subtractions of different lengths. In the folds it is a problem with the same answer length whose answer differs at both tokens.
The numbers

The paper's scorer, rerun now, reproduces its numbers exactly: elicit parent 2.31, format parent 0.06, base 0.02. On the folds the preference disappears. The elicit parent scores −0.07 ± 0.13 nats on the paper version and +0.06 ± 0.34 on the full-answer version, right about half the time, and is not above the format parent (4/10 and 6/10 folds; neither significant). The original 256 problems give the same (≈ 0.03). So the 2.31 most likely came from the parent knowing the answer's rough length, size or sign, which a same-length wrong answer removes. It did not come from knowing the answer itself. Which of these factors carries it has not been tested.

Symbol form (added 2026-11-05) is the positive control, not a hidden preference. The elicit parent already answers symbol sums (40% first token correct), and there it prefers the right answer on 99% of problems, on all 10 folds. So the scorer does detect a model that knows the answer. In word form that knowledge does not reach the first answer token. The format parent and base show nothing in either form.

This was meant to be the cheapest of the four metrics (no fine-tuning, no circuit) and the baseline the circuit metrics should beat. As measured here it does not separate the parents, so it can't serve as that baseline.

10-fold check · 2026-11-04

How robust

What was repeated
  • The single split above is one draw of 512 + 256 problems. The check drew 10 new folds, each with its own 512 discovery and 256 test problems plus its own pool of wrong-answer partners. No problem appears in two folds, in the original split or in any training file.
  • Folds are stratified by answer length and number of carries, so each fold has the same mix.
  • Same models, same code. Rerunning the original split reproduced its saved scores exactly.
  • Every fold gives one paired (elicit, teach) value per metric and size. Test: paired t-test over the 10 folds, Holm-corrected across sizes. A metric "holds" at a size if elicit beats teach on at least 9 of 10 folds and the corrected p < 0.05 (fixed before the run).
Result
  • Edge overlap, functional reuse, CMAP: hold at every size, 10/10 folds, no fold where the gap flips. Gaps (95% interval): overlap 0.26–0.47 (each ±0.01–0.05), functional share 0.56–0.68 (±0.04–0.06), CMAP recovery 1.73–2.12 (±0.03–0.07).
  • The original split's numbers fall inside the fold range everywhere except functional reuse at 0.2% (0.92 vs 0.71–0.89).
  • The teach route is significantly above the base floor at every size (by 0.04–0.16), as before: a little shared with its parent beyond pretraining, not nothing.
  • Hidden preference fails (see above).

What the folds do not cover: training randomness (one run per child), other prompt templates, other tasks.

Caveats

The taught child learned the task much worse

96% vs 11% on fresh 4-digit problems (95.3% vs 14.5% on the original 256). Part of every route difference may be "the teach child failed" rather than "teach builds a new circuit". The taught child's own circuit is still stable (half-split ceiling 0.53–0.84; across folds 0.62–0.87), so its low overlap is not just noise.

One training run per child

The fold intervals cover which problems were sampled, not training randomness. A retrained child could differ.

The elicit route separates only through the symbol form

Scored on the word form, the elicit parent's circuit overlaps the elicited child at 0.26–0.67, about the teach route's level. The parent never answers the word task, so the symbol form is where its addition circuit lives. Functional reuse shows the same: its word-form circuit carries 28–55% of the child's behaviour, vs 79–98% for the symbol-form circuit.

The format parent has no task circuit to keep

Removing all of its edges moves its output by only 0.005 nats, so it fails the gate for the circuit tests. Its "circuit" is a fairly stable ranking (ceiling 0.74–0.86 within a split, 0.52–0.77 across folds) of edges with almost no effect. That is what a teach parent should look like, but it means the teach-route overlap compares against a circuit that does nothing.

No circuit passes every validation test

Partial necessity passes at every size. Sufficiency passes from 0.1% up for every gated model (from 0.05% for the elicit pair). Equivalence passes only for the elicit pair at 2–5%. Consistency and specificity fail at every size for every model: the top edges are shared machinery, also needed for a simple copy task. (These tests were not repeated on the folds.)
