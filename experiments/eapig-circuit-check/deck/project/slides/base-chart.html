The 2026-10-28 run adds 0.01%, 0.02%, 0.05% (k = 20, 39, 98 edges). At 0.01–0.02% the base shares 0.48–0.50 with the elicit child and 0.25–0.30 with the teach child, higher than the ~0.2 floor at 0.5–5%; at 0.05% 0.36 and 0.41. The elicit route (parent op to child, 0.81–0.91) stays far above base at every size. The teach route (0.39–0.50) is no higher than the control (0.35–0.53); at 0.01% it is above base to teach child (0.25) but below base to elicit child (0.48). Caution at k = 20: Jaccard moves in coarse steps (0.905 = 19 of 20 edges) and intervals are wide (base to teach child 0.250 with basic interval 0.167–0.250), so do not read differences among the three tiny sizes as trends. The base TinyStories-1B model is the common ancestor of all four models. It was scored with the same KL EAP-IG on the same 512 NL prompts. Its circuit shares 0.20–0.34 of edges with the elicit child and 0.21–0.37 with the teach child: far above chance (8–100×), so shared pretrained plumbing gives a floor of about 0.2. Any two arithmetic-tuned relatives share about 0.3 (format parent → teach child, the control, elicit parent NL → elicit child). The elicit op-form parent → child pair, 0.50–0.77, is far above both tiers at every size. The teach child shares more with its format parent than with the base (by 0.04–0.10 from 0.2% up), but no more than with the unrelated control parent. The base's own split-half ceiling is the lowest of all models from 0.5% up (0.48–0.57), which explains part of its lower overlap; adjusted for ceilings, base → teach child sits only 0.02–0.09 below format parent → teach child.
