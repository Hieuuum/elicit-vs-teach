Recovery = 1 − KL(child‖patched) ÷ KL(child‖parent). The parent runs with the child's activations on the child's circuit edges. Thick blue: the elicit child's circuit moves the parent almost all the way to the child (0.52 at 0.01% to 0.98 at 5%; accuracy from 0 to 0.05–0.86). Thin blue with open rings: the child's circuit edges that are NOT in the parent's op-form circuit do no better than the null at 0.05% and above (-0.02 to 0.18), so the effect rides on the shared edges (0.68–0.97, in the table on the next slide). At 0.01–0.02% the shared edges carry most (0.44, 0.56), but the not-shared edges recover 0.18 and 0.36; only about 2–4 child edges are not shared at these sizes, so treat that as a small-set effect. Orange: the teach child's circuit pushes the format parent further from the child than it started (−0.83 to −1.19 from 0.1%; −0.58 at 0.05%; about 0 at 0.01–0.02%: 0.02, −0.07). The gray dotted null is the higher of the two routes' disjoint type-matched nulls at each size. It is smaller than the circuit at small sizes (elicit 6, 15, 19 of 17, 35, 92 edges at 0.01, 0.02, 0.05%; 71 of 188 at 0.1%); a uniform random null stays at or below 0.004.
