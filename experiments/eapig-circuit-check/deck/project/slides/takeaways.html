Takeaways

Elicit child reuses its parent’s circuit

Overlap 0.72–0.91 at 0.01–1% (84–102% of the ceiling; 19 of the top 20 edges shared); the parent’s circuit alone does 86–92% of the child’s circuit’s work. Only the op-form circuit: the NL circuit overlaps 0.31–0.42, the teach level.

Teach child keeps about a third

Overlap 0.25–0.50 (41–60% of the ceiling), share 0.24–0.41. Its parent has almost no addition signal to keep.

Patching the child’s circuit into the parent splits the routes

Elicit recovers 0.52–0.98 of the child, rising with size (accuracy 0 → 0.05–0.86), mostly through edges shared with the op-form circuit. Teach is ≈0 at 0.01–0.02%, then negative (−0.58 to −1.19): the parent moves away.

No circuit passes all five tests

2/5 almost everywhere; 3/5 only for the elicit pair at 2–5%. Consistency and specificity never pass. At 0.01–0.02% sufficiency fails too (20–39 edges alone recover nothing), but removing them still wipes out the behavior.

The small sizes repeat the 2–5% result from 2026-10-08, and the tiny sizes (0.01–0.05%, 20–98 edges, 2026-10-28) extend it: overlap and patching keep the same split; functional shares cannot be computed there because every circuit recovers f ≈ 0. The elicit route keeps the parent’s op-form circuit nearly whole; the teach route overlaps its parent no more than the control does. Child → parent patching (2026-10-22) agrees: the elicit child’s circuit edges, run through the parent’s own downstream weights, produce the child’s answers, and only the edges shared with the op-form circuit do the work; the teach child’s circuit pushes the format parent away from the child. The test failures are shared by every model: the top edges are general machinery (removing them breaks the copy task too), and per-problem circuits differ a lot from the shared one.
