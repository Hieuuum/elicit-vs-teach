Takeaways

Elicit child reuses its parent’s circuit

Overlap 0.72–0.77 at 0.1–1% (84–91% of the ceiling); the parent’s circuit alone does 86–92% of the child’s circuit’s work. Only the op-form circuit: the NL circuit overlaps 0.31–0.42, the teach level.

Teach child keeps about a third

Overlap 0.25–0.43 (41–51% of the ceiling), share 0.24–0.41. Its parent has almost no addition signal to keep.

Patching the child’s circuit into the parent splits the routes

Elicit recovers 0.77–0.98 of the child (accuracy 0 → 0.32–0.86), through edges shared with the op-form circuit. Teach goes negative (−0.83 to −1.19): the parent moves away.

No circuit passes all five tests

2/5 almost everywhere; 3/5 only for the elicit pair at 2–5%. Consistency and specificity never pass.

The small sizes repeat the 2–5% result from 2026-10-08. The elicit route keeps the parent’s op-form circuit nearly whole; the teach route overlaps its parent no more than the control does. Child → parent patching (2026-10-22) agrees: the elicit child’s circuit edges, run through the parent’s own downstream weights, produce the child’s answers, and only the edges shared with the op-form circuit do the work; the teach child’s circuit pushes the format parent away from the child. The test failures are shared by every model: the top edges are general machinery (removing them breaks the copy task too), and per-problem circuits differ a lot from the shared one.
